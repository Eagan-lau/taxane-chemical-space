# Supplementary Information

## A provenance-resolved enzymatic reaction grammar delineates the generative chemical space of taxanes

**Authors:** AUTHOR_INPUT_REQUIRED

## Scope

This Supplementary Information contains frozen supporting analyses for the
four-figure manuscript. No reaction rule, generated structure, fingerprint,
descriptor, benchmark outcome, convergence assignment, or bridge assignment
was recalculated during editorial assembly. Permitted operations were source
data extraction, panel recomposition, table consolidation, document
rendering, and consistency auditing.

Generated structures are reaction-grammar-accessible hypotheses and are not
reported as observed metabolites. The grammar represents transferable
chemical edits and does not assign enzymes. Curated Taxol-pathway productions
remain within T1 with explicit provenance; their replay measures domain
calibration, not independent pathway prediction. G1 and G2 are the primary
near-seed neighborhood, whereas G3 is exploratory and right-censored.
Grammar depth is not necessarily equivalent to biochemical enzyme-step count.

## Supplementary data organization

The original 15 supplementary figures were consolidated into eight thematic
figures. Redundant membership, decorative gallery, bridge-network, and route
panels were omitted where the same result is represented more directly in a
main figure or a retained complete supplementary panel. Their original files
remain in the release source-data archive.

The original 22 supplementary tables were consolidated into 11 logical table
groups. Each workbook sheet may contain several labelled component tables
with independent, checksum-bearing TSV files. The compact bridge summary was
removed because the complete candidate and directed-pair tables are retained.
Large authoritative molecular and event tables remain separate from Excel to
avoid worksheet row limits or silent truncation.

## Supplementary tables

- **Table S1. Reaction provenance, rule-build attrition, evidence tiers, and
  pathway calibration.** S1A reports source provenance; S1B reports build
  attrition and tier definitions; S1C reports independent one-step outputs;
  S1D reports G1 set overlap; and S1E reports leakage-controlled and
  domain-informed pathway evaluations.
- **Table S2. Primary T1 grammar and G0 activation.** S2A contains the
  74-production T1 grammar provenance and quality-control fields; S2B contains
  the G0 rule and matching-site activation audit.
- **Table S3. Generation-resolved chemical-space expansion.** Complete G0-G3
  counts of structures, events, raw products, rejections, reconnections,
  reverse cycles, and active rules.
- **Table S4. Authoritative data-object manifests.** S4A-S4D describe the
  complete molecular-state, directed-event, parent-rule application, and
  product-rejection tables.
- **Table S5. Quality control, robustness, and computational performance.**
  S5A-S5C contain the frozen QC comparison, parameter robustness analysis,
  and computational performance summary.
- **Table S6. Physicochemical descriptors and nearest-G0 similarity.**
  Generation-resolved descriptor and fingerprint-similarity summaries.
- **Table S7. Structure-derived functional-state transitions.** Transition
  counts recalculated from accepted source and product structures.
- **Table S8. Grammar use and reaction edits.** S8A reports exact-rule and
  semantic-group concentration; S8B reports edit locality and elemental
  formula deltas.
- **Table S9. Convergence and route multiplicity.** First-observation parent
  convergence and associated path-support summaries.
- **Table S10. All latent bridge candidates.** Complete records for the 227
  conservative G1/G2 bridge intermediates.
- **Table S11. All directed G0-pair bridge records.** Complete records for the
  309 ordered known-taxane pairs supported by bridge intermediates.

Complete G0-G3 molecular states, including SMILES, are distributed as
generation-specific CSV files containing 648, 15,801, 223,823, and 2,362,766
records, respectively.
